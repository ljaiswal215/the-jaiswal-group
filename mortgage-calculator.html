UNDERSTANDING YOUR PAYMENT

UNDERSTANDING THE TRUE COST OF BUYING A HOME IN SAN DIEGO

A mortgage payment is more than just principal and interest. When buying a home in San Diego, it's important to understand the full monthly cost of ownership — including property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA dues, and Mello-Roos. Our mortgage calculator helps estimate your monthly payment so you can budget more confidently and make informed decisions.

If you want personalized guidance, we also work closely with trusted San Diego mortgage lenders and lending partners who can help you explore financing options, estimate cash-to-close, and determine what loan program may be the best fit for your goals.

Principal & Interest (P&I)

Principal and interest form the foundation of your mortgage payment. The principal is the amount borrowed that reduces your loan balance over time, while interest is the cost of borrowing from your lender.

Your monthly principal and interest payment is based on:

  • Purchase price
  • Down payment
  • Interest rate
  • Loan term (typically 15 or 30 years)

For fixed-rate mortgages, this portion of your payment remains stable for the life of the loan, making it one of the most predictable parts of your housing payment.

Property Taxes in San Diego

Property taxes are one of the largest ongoing costs of owning a home in California. In most cases, San Diego property taxes start at approximately 1% of the assessed value, plus voter-approved local assessments, bonds, and special taxes.

In reality, the effective property tax rate in San Diego County often ranges between 1.1% and 1.3%, depending on the city, neighborhood, and whether the home carries additional obligations like Mello-Roos.

Our calculator defaults to 1.14%, but this should be adjusted based on the property you're considering.

What Is Proposition 13?

Proposition 13 is one of the most important laws affecting California property taxes. Passed in 1978, it limits the base property tax rate to 1% of a home's assessed value and caps annual increases in assessed value at no more than 2% per year.

This means:

  • Your assessed value starts at your purchase price
  • Property taxes grow slowly over time
  • Long-term owners often pay significantly less in taxes than new buyers purchasing at today's market values

When a property sells, the tax basis resets. This is why buyers in San Diego should calculate property taxes based on their purchase price — not what the current owner is paying.

What Are Supplemental Property Taxes?

Many California home buyers are surprised by supplemental property taxes.

When you buy a home, the county reassesses the property at your purchase price. If the seller's tax basis was lower, you may receive a separate supplemental tax bill covering the difference for the remainder of the fiscal year.

This is:

  • A one-time charge
  • Billed directly by the county
  • Often not included in your lender's escrow setup

Depending on your closing date, you may receive one or even two supplemental tax bills. This is an important upfront cost many buyers overlook.

Assessed Value vs. Market Value

Understanding the difference between market value and assessed value is critical.

Market value is what a home is worth in today's market based on comparable sales, demand, condition, and location.

Assessed value is what the county uses to calculate your property taxes.

In California, Proposition 13 keeps assessed value growth limited — even when market value rises significantly. This creates one of the long-term financial advantages of homeownership in California.

Homeowners Insurance

Most lenders require homeowners insurance in San Diego, and it's typically collected as part of your monthly mortgage payment through escrow.

Insurance costs vary based on:

  • Home value
  • Replacement cost
  • Fire zone exposure
  • Age of home
  • Coverage levels

In San Diego County, many buyers can expect homeowners insurance costs to range between $100–$250 per month, though homes in wildfire-prone areas may be higher.

If you're comparing properties, our trusted lender partners can help estimate insurance costs more accurately based on your target neighborhood.

HOA Dues

Many homes in San Diego — especially in master-planned communities like Pacific Highlands Ranch, Carmel Valley, Del Sur, Rancho Tesoro, and Otay Ranch — have monthly HOA dues.

HOA fees may cover:

  • Community maintenance
  • Pools and clubhouses
  • Gated security
  • Landscaping
  • Recreational amenities

HOA dues can vary significantly by neighborhood and should always be factored into your monthly budget. If you're searching homes in a specific community, we can help provide current HOA information.

What Is Mello-Roos?

Mello-Roos is a special tax assessment commonly found in newer California communities and master-planned developments. It helps fund infrastructure like schools, parks, roads, and utilities.

Unlike regular property taxes, Mello-Roos is separate and can add anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand dollars annually to your ownership costs.

In North County San Diego, Mello-Roos is common in communities like:

  • Pacific Highlands Ranch
  • 4S Ranch / Del Sur
  • Santaluz
  • San Elijo Hills
  • Bressi Ranch

Before making an offer, it's important to understand the total tax picture — and we can help you evaluate that.
